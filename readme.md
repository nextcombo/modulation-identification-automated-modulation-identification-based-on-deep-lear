# [Modulation Identification] Automated Modulation Identification Based on Deep Learning in OTFS and OFDM Systems [with code attached]

Introduction: Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) is a promising broadband communication modulation scheme in 6G technology [1] and [2]. Its superior performance over Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) in handling most Doppler shifts and Doppler spread makes OTFS an appropriate alternative for high mobility scenarios [3]. However, its high complexity restricts its further application, especially in low mobility scenarios [4]. Therefore, the coexistence of OTFS and OFDM signals within the same system will be a practical trade-off between performance and complexity. From another perspective, a simplified and effective method to design OTFS transmitters is to add precoding units to the multicarrier OFDM modulator. Feasible solutions for implementing both schemes in the same architecture have been proposed in [5] and [6]. Additionally, [7] presents TDD and FDD schemes for OTFS and OFDM waveforms in cooperative communication scenarios. However, there is a lack of relevant research on identifying both schemes in non-cooperative communication systems. In fact, without automatic recognition of these two modulation schemes, OTFS may not be widely applied in civilian and military fields.
Automatic Modulation Recognition (AMR) plays a crucial role in non-cooperative communication scenarios, including spectrum monitoring, cognitive radio, and the security of communication systems. It primarily consists of two methods: likelihood-based (LB) and probability-based (FB) approaches [8]. The former is mainly based on hypothesis testing, which has high computational complexity and requires additional prior knowledge of fading channels [9]. The latter is a low-complexity FB method that includes feature extraction and classification techniques, gaining widespread attention from both academic and industrial communities in recent years. The core step in the FB method is to extract effective features for input into an appropriate classifier. Higher order cumulants (HOCs) are one of the most popular features in AMR due to their advantages in combating multipath fading and low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [10]. Additionally, some researchers have applied cyclic and instantaneous features to AMR tasks [11]. Support Vector Machines (SVMs) are representative traditional machine learning (ML) classifiers in the physical layer for classifying modulation modes of signals [12]. With the booming development of deep learning (DL), many studies have started to focus on neural network design for AMC tasks. DL-based methods typically directly process raw data to avoid additional costs and expertise in signal processing. Moreover, DL-based methods often exhibit better performance in terms of classification accuracy compared to traditional methods. This paper introduces a CNN-based modulation format classification method, which takes in-phase and quadrature samples as well as constellation diagrams as inputs. The residual network proposed in [14] has achieved great success in the image processing field due to its ability to prevent overfitting. Inspired by this, many studies adopt residual units to improve the performance of their proposed methods [15].
Recently, numerous studies have investigated the AMR tasks in specific application scenarios, such as OFDM systems. Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is one of the most widely used universal modulation methods in wireless communication systems. [16] proposed an AMR algorithm based on LB in a wireless time-division duplex OFDM system. In [17], the OFDM scheme was distinguished from other modulation modes. The different modulation orders of subcarriers in the OFDM system were identified based on a method based on CNN proposed in [18]. However, as another important modulation scheme, the OTFS in the AMC field has not been studied yet. The ability to recognize received signals without prior knowledge is a fundamental factor for large-scale communication systems. This paper proposes a new deep learning-based method for identifying modulation schemes of OTFS and OFDM signals. The experimental results validate the effectiveness of the method.
The main contributions of our work can be summarized as follows:
·To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work on identifying OTFS and OFDM modulation schemes under non-cooperative scenarios.
·We proposed an improved Compressed Sensing Encode-and-Decode Network (SE-CNN), which is used to classify two schemes under six different subcarrier modulation orders and other common modulation methods. To combat multipath and Doppler spread in real OTFS application scenarios with harsh channel conditions, the SE module is adopted in this paper. Both signal data structures in the two schemes are well designed to ensure fairness.
·The effectiveness of the proposed method is verified by extensive simulation results. The results show that compared to other effective machine learning methods, the proposed method has certain advantages. Under different datasets and different Doppler spread values, the model has good robustness.

Experiment Simulation A. Design of OTFS and OFDM Signals
The OTFS (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) and OFDM (Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing) schemes are actually two types of communication solutions. The design of the data frame structure in both OTFS and OFDM systems may have a significant impact on the accuracy of our proposed model. For fairness, it is necessary that the length of the data samples be the same to ensure uniform power between the two types of signals. At the same time, the number of subcarriers M in both OFDM and OTFS systems must be consistent. Given these circumstances, we set M to 64 and N to 8. Both schemes have a CP (Cyclic Prefix) length of 16. In the single sample of the OTFS signal, there are 4 subframes, and the sum of the data samples is (64 + 16) × 8 × 4 = 2560. For OFDM, there are 32 subframes. The sum of the data samples is (64 + 16) × 32 = 2560, which equals the length of the OTFS samples.

B. Simulation results
In the experiment, data generation was completed using MATLAB. The NVIDIA GeForce GTX 2080 Ti is suitable for training and testing networks. The implementation of the network is based on Pytorch. To evaluate the performance of the proposed SE-CNN model, we prepared six datasets θi (i = 1, 2 ..., 6). The ratio of training set and test set is 7:3. The SNR ranges from −10 dB to 14 dB with an interval of 2 dB. The OTFS and OFDM signals in the dataset correspond to six different subcarrier modulations including BPSK, QPSK, 8PSK, 16QAM, 64QAM and 256QAM. For each type of subcarrier modulation, there are 3000 data samples.

The dataset θ1 includes five modulation modes: OTFS, OFDM, 2ASK, PAM4, and BPSK, each with 18000 data samples. The maximum Doppler shift in θ1 is set at 1400 Hz. As shown in Figure 5, a reasonable comparison between the proposed method and popular classification methods, including DrCNN[13] for IQ samples, DrCNN for AP samples, SVM for HOC, and DNN for HOC, was conducted. With an increase in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the classification accuracy of the proposed method also continuously improved. Finally, around an SNR of 8 dB, the accuracy approached 100%. DrCNN consists of five convolutional layers and fully connected layers, which were fine-tuned to compare with the proposed model. Experimental results show that the proposed method has higher accuracy and recall rates. In the case of poor fading channels, it can be observed that the accuracy of IQ samples is higher than that of AP samples. This can be explained as IQ samples being a better input for the AMR task compared to AP samples. Experimental results indicate that regardless of the choice of machine learning method as a classifier, traditional HOC-based methods perform poorly in OTFS and OFDM AMR tasks. The results also demonstrate the advantages of using raw samples and deep learning methods over manufacturing features.

Furthermore, confusion matrices for -2 dB, 2 dB, 6 dB, and 10 dB are shown in Figure 6. We can observe that our model can identify all five modulation modes with high accuracy. Moreover, among other modulation modes, OTFS achieved the highest precision. We speculate this phenomenon is related to OTFS's Doppler resistance.
To make the experimental environment more similar to real-world OTFS application scenarios, we have studied the impact of maximum Doppler shifts on our model. For one or more datasets (θi, i = 2, 3, ..., ..., ..., ..., ..., ..., ..., ..., ...) in which only OFDM signals and OTFS signals are present. Other conditions are similar to those of dataset θ1. According to the trend shown in Figure 7, our model's performance improves with an increase in signal-to-noise ratio. The results indicate that the anti-Doppler property of OTFS signals primarily resides within the OTFS receiver, hence in non-cooperative communication systems, Doppler broadening still negatively impacts accuracy. Although larger Doppler shifts can cause certain degradation to system performance, our algorithm remains feasible. At a signal-to-noise ratio close to 10dB, the accuracy of our performance model reaches 100%. This verifies the robustness of the proposed method.
